# «Сердце синего кита» — Watercolor Brush · treatment

Vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, Russian voice (ElevenLabs, arrives later) + Russian subtitles on the paper + `.srt`.
Channel «Факты за минуту». ~45 s. Style: `styles/watercolor` (a naturalist's journal page that paints itself; brush strokes with dry-brush flying white on warm cotton paper; hand-written notes with leader lines).

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Journal pages.** Every fact a new specimen plate (heart, tongue, artery…), turned like pages. Clear, but static and list-like.
2. **One ocean, one sheet (chosen).** The whole film lives on one tall painted sea: the whale paints itself under sun rays; the camera dollies along its 30 m body past the things it is compared with, tilts down with it into the dark (the heart slows) and back up into the light (the heart races); specimen plates (heart on a scale, tongue vs elephant, the artery and the plate) open as journal inserts over the sea; the song spreads as rings across the whole sheet; at sunset the tail lifts and the page washes to paper.
3. **Anatomy of one object.** Only the heart, dissected into labelled parts. Elegant, but the script is about the whole animal.

**Why 2:** the dive is literally a vertical tilt — the medium's "palette as data" (the sea darkens band by band with depth) carries the heartbeat fact; size comparisons become a transect along the body.

## 2. Logline and arc

How big is the heart of the biggest animal that ever lived — and what does it do in the dark?
- Setup: the whale paints itself, a vermilion heart glows inside it — «≈ 180 кг».
- Turn: scale (people, a dinosaur, a bus and a house), the dive (2 beats a minute) and the return (37), the tongue and the artery myth.
- Ending: the song crosses the whole sheet; sunset tail; the page dries back to paper with the channel name.

## 3. Benchmark

- Naturalist field journals and specimen plates; textbook transect diagrams (grammar only).
- **Learn:** one hero per view with its hand-written note; structure first, detail second; palette as data (depth bands).
- **Don't take:** any specific painting, illustrator's character or layout.

## 6. Cue map

72 BPM (beat 0.833 s), F major, felt piano + kalimba + soft string pad + glockenspiel.
- Opening: piano motif, pad swells as the whale paints.
- Dive: music thins to a low pad; one heartbeat thump every ~2 bars (stylised "2 a minute"); **near-silence** at the bottom.
- Ascent: kalimba returns, heartbeats speed up.
- Plates: glockenspiel accents on each note/stamp.
- Song: whale-song glides over a held chord; final cadence on the sunset.

## 8. Type and subtitles

Cormorant Garamond (titles, italic subtitles on a paper halo, ink at ~78 %), Caveat for hand notes with leader lines, IBM Plex Mono for measured labels (180 кг, 30 м, 2 / 37 уд/мин, 23 см). Numbers as digits on screen. One accent colour: vermilion for the heart, the artery and the myth cross.

## Facts used

- Blue whale heart ≈ 180 kg (measured specimen, Royal Ontario Museum, 2015).
- Largest animal known to have lived; length up to ~30 m.
- Heart rate 2 bpm at depth, up to 37 bpm at the surface — Goldbogen et al., PNAS 2019.
- Tongue mass ~2.7 t, comparable to an elephant — commonly cited (NOAA / natural-history references).
- Aorta diameter ~23 cm; "a person could swim through its arteries" is a myth.
- Songs travel hundreds of kilometres (low-frequency calls).
