# «Молния горячее Солнца» — Watercolor Brush · treatment

Vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, Russian voice (ElevenLabs, arrives later) + subtitles on the paper + `.srt`. ~45 s.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Specimen plates only** — every fact a locked journal page. Clear but static.
2. **One storm, kept in a field journal (chosen).** The storm arrives over painted fields; between strikes the journal opens plates that measure it (thermometers, five suns, the 6 s ÷ 3 map, the fulgurite); the storm passes and a rainbow paints itself.
3. **A countdown** from flash to thunder, each second one fact. Clever, but forces the facts into one gimmick.

**Why 2:** the medium's white is the paper — a lightning flash is literally unpainted paper flaring through the washes; the storm → rainbow arc gives an ending that echoes the opening fields.

## Arc
Setup: a bolt over dark fields — hotter than the Sun's surface. Turn: measure it (5 500 vs 30 000 °C, ×5), hear it (thunder rings), use it (count ÷ 3), count them (≈ 40 per second), watch it repeat (a tower struck three times), hold its trace (fulgurite). Ending: storm passes, rainbow, page washes to paper.

## Sound
Rain bed in storm scenes, a crack on every strike, thunder rolling in after a delay, kalimba ticks on the count, glockenspiel sparkles over the Earth, wind on the sand, birds and a cadence under the rainbow. Score: felt piano / kalimba / pad / glockenspiel; storm scenes only a low pad.

## Type
Cormorant Garamond titles and italic subtitles, Caveat hand notes, IBM Plex Mono values (5 500 °C, 30 000 °C, ×5, 6 с ÷ 3 = 2 км, ≈ 40). Accent vermilion: the lightning thermometer, the count, the tally.

## Facts used
Sun photosphere ≈ 5 500 °C; lightning channel up to ≈ 30 000 °C (NOAA/NWS); thunder from rapid expansion of heated air; distance ≈ seconds ÷ 3 in km; ≈ 40–50 flashes per second worldwide (NASA); repeated strikes on tall structures; fulgurites from strikes in sand.
