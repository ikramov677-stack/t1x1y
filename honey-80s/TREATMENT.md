# «Мёд почти не портится» — 80s Cel Anime · treatment

Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, Russian voice (ElevenLabs, arrives later) + burned-in Russian subtitles + `.srt`.
Channel: «Факты за минуту». ~45 s. Style: `styles/cel-anime-80s` (flat two-tone cels over airbrushed plates, backlit light, VHS on a CRT).

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Mock anime opening.** Every fact is a "character" introduced with a pose and a name card (Мёд, Вода, Бактерия, Пчела…), city-pop all the way. Fun, but it turns facts into a list and the topic into a roster — the style's cliché.
2. **The jar that waited (chosen).** One object, one journey: a jar of honey on a kitchen table at golden hour → we fall back 3000 years into a torchlit tomb where an archaeologist, Аня, opens a sealed clay jar and the honey glows *through* the frame. Her field notebook turns the science into inserts (water, bacteria, acid, enzyme, the bee). The film ends where it began, at the same table, the next morning: the jar has crystallised — and it is fine.
3. **Before / after, one place, two hours.** The kitchen at dusk and at dawn, only the honey changes. Elegant, but the tomb — the best image of the script — would be lost.

**Why 2:** the hook (thousands of years) becomes a place we visit, not a number; the backlit honey is the style's native move at the emotional peak; the bookended kitchen jar (liquid → crystallised) pays off the last fact visually.

## 2. Logline and arc

A jar of honey older than the pyramids' fame is opened — and it is still good; we find out why.
- Setup: a modern jar, golden, backlit — "тысячи лет?!"
- Turn: tomb, the clay jar opens, light pours out; the "why" unfolds in Anya's notebook (water ✕, bacteria leave, acid, enzyme shield, the tiny spoon gag with a tape rewind).
- Ending: back to the kitchen next morning (new colour model), the crystallised jar beside a liquid one — normal. A bee flies out of the window into a sunny garden; end card.

## 3. Benchmark

- 1980s Japanese TV/OVA slice-of-life openings and "eyecatch" bumpers (grammar only).
- **Learn:** painted plate vs flat cel contrast; held cels moved by light bands; insert shots with bokeh and sliding highlight; colour models per hour (dusk kitchen, torch tomb, morning kitchen, noon garden); eyecatch cards between "acts".
- **Don't take:** any character design, logo, melody, shot or title design of an existing work.

## 6. Cue map

City-pop, 100 BPM (beat 0.6 s, bar 2.4 s), key D♭ major (D♭maj7 – B♭m9 – G♭maj7 – A♭7sus).
- Intro (shot 1): FM e-piano chord + bell sparkle, brass stab on the title.
- Tomb (2–3): drums drop out, low string pad + torch crackle; **one beat of true silence** before the lid; on the glow: full chord + bell + gated snare.
- Science (4–8): groove — drum machine, slap bass, 16th guitar chanks, e-piano.
- Spoon (9): groove continues → **tape stop** (pitch-down 0.4 s, silence) on «нет» → restarts with a fill.
- Kitchen morning (10): softer, e-piano + bass only.
- Garden/end (11): last chorus a semitone up (D major), sax-like synth lead, ends on a held chord.
Cuts land on beats; scene starts are snapped to the beat grid when the voice timing is known.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles on the tape (inside the CRT pass): PT Sans Narrow Bold, cream-yellow, thick dark outline + soft shadow, ~54 px, lower area clear of key action (vertical frame: baseline ~1640 px).
- Titles: Russo One, slanted, chrome gradient (sky → white horizon → violet → pink → gold) with dark + neon outlines, flicker-on at 12 fps, light sweep and star glint; a small katakana line ハチミツ ("honey") in Dela Gothic One on the opening title and the end card.
- Cards: «ТЫСЯЧИ ЛЕТ?!», «НЕ ИСПОРТИЛСЯ», «МАЛО ВОДЫ», «pH ≈ 3.9», «ФЕРМЕНТ», «½ ЛОЖКИ?» → «1/12!», «НОРМА ✓», end card «ФАКТЫ ЗА МИНУТУ».

## Facts used

- Edible honey found in ancient Egyptian tombs (thousands of years old) — widely reported (e.g. Smithsonian Magazine, "The Science Behind Honey's Eternal Shelf Life", 2013).
- Low water (~17–18 %), acidity pH ≈ 3.2–4.5 (≈ 3.9 average), glucose oxidase → hydrogen peroxide — same source.
- A worker bee makes about 1/12 teaspoon of honey in its lifetime — commonly cited by beekeeping associations.
- Crystallisation is normal and not spoilage.
